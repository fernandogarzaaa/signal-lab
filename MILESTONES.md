# Milestones

Each milestone lists its goal, the contract, and the acceptance criteria that
mark it done. Build in order; each one feeds the next. After finishing a
milestone, write a one-page note in `docs/mN-writeup.md` covering: problem,
data, approach, metrics, what you would do with more time. Those notes are
your interview script.

---

## M1: News + price data pipeline

**Goal.** Reproducible ingestion of financial news and market prices into a
local analytical store.

**Contract.**
- `signal_lab.ingest.news.fetch(query, start, end)`: pull news via the GDELT
  2.1 DOC API (`https://api.gdeltproject.org/api/v2/doc/doc`), no API key
  needed. Store raw JSON plus a normalized table: `url, title, body_snippet,
  published_at, source_domain`.
- `signal_lab.ingest.prices.fetch(tickers, start, end)`: pull daily OHLCV via
  yfinance. Store table: `ticker, date, open, high, low, close, volume`.
- Storage: DuckDB file at `data/signal_lab.duckdb`. Reruns are idempotent
  (upsert on natural keys, never duplicate rows).
- CLI: `python -m signal_lab.ingest.run --tickers AAPL,MSFT,JPM --days 90`.

**Acceptance.**
- 90 days of prices for 10 tickers and at least 2,000 news articles land in
  DuckDB with correct schemas.
- Rerunning the CLI changes nothing (row counts identical).
- A SQL query joining news dates to price dates runs in under a second.

**Interview asset.** "Tell us about analysing a large dataset": you describe
source selection, schema design, idempotency, and the join key (date + entity).

## M2: Entity extraction (PermID-lite)

**Goal.** Map company mentions in news text to tickers, the way LSEG maps
unstructured text to PermID identifiers.

**Contract.**
- `signal_lab.nlp.entities.extract(text) -> list[(mention, ticker, confidence)]`.
- Start with a deterministic baseline: alias dictionary
  (`data/aliases.csv`: "Apple" -> AAPL, "JPMorgan" -> JPM, ...) plus simple
  matching. Then upgrade to a real NER model (spaCy `en_core_web_sm` or a
  transformers NER pipeline) and compare.
- Ambiguity handling: "Apple" the company vs the fruit; mention your rule.

**Acceptance.**
- Hand-label 100 headlines; report precision/recall/F1 for baseline vs NER.
- The NER version beats the baseline by a clear margin, and you can explain
  three error cases by hand.

**Interview asset.** Entity extraction is named in the job ad. You walk them
through baseline-first thinking and error analysis.

## M3: Sentiment classifier with classical ML

**Goal.** Classify news items as market-moving-positive, neutral, or
market-moving-negative using classical models, on imbalanced data.

**Contract.**
- Labels: derive weak labels from next-day abnormal returns (top/bottom
  deciles), or hand-label 500 items. Document the labeling choice and its bias.
- Models: logistic regression (TF-IDF) as baseline, then gradient boosting
  (LightGBM or XGBoost). No LLMs here; this milestone is classical ML theory.
- Handle imbalance explicitly: class weights vs resampling vs threshold tuning.
  Compare all three.
- Metrics: precision, recall, F1, and PR-AUC (not accuracy). Calibration curve.

**Acceptance.**
- PR-AUC clearly beats a majority-class baseline; you can state by how much.
- You can explain, from first principles: why PR-AUC over ROC-AUC here, what
  class weights do to the loss, and how threshold tuning trades precision for
  recall.
- `tests/test_models.py` asserts the training pipeline runs end to end on a
  tiny fixture.

**Interview asset.** This is the reported LSEG case question almost verbatim:
"How would you build a model to predict customer churn when the dataset is
highly imbalanced?" Your answer is this milestone, with numbers.

## M4: Event study (classical statistics)

**Goal.** Answer with statistics: do sentiment spikes predict abnormal returns?

**Contract.**
- `signal_lab.stats.event_study(events, prices)`: for each high-sentiment event
  day, compute abnormal return vs a market benchmark over windows [-1,+1],
  [-1,+5]. Report mean abnormal return, t-statistic, p-value, confidence
  interval.
- Plot the distribution of abnormal returns; check normality assumptions and
  say what breaks if they fail.
- Multiple events per company: address overlapping windows honestly.

**Acceptance.**
- A table of events with t-stats, p-values, and 95% CIs, generated by code.
- You can explain: null hypothesis, what the p-value actually means, why you
  used a t-test, and one way this analysis could lie to you.

**Interview asset.** The reported LSEG statistics probe: "How will you
incorporate Statistics to the Data Science field?" Your answer is this
milestone, not a textbook definition.

## M5: Walk-forward backtest (experimental design)

**Goal.** Compare trading-ish strategies driven by your signals without
fooling yourself.

**Contract.**
- `signal_lab.backtest.run(signals, prices)`: walk-forward splits, strategies
  compared: sentiment-momentum vs baseline (buy-and-hold) vs random-entry
  control. Include transaction costs.
- No lookahead: features at time t may only use information available at t.
  Write a test that fails if a future timestamp leaks into a feature.
- Compare strategies with a paired test or bootstrap on the Sharpe difference;
  note the multiple-comparisons problem when trying many variants.

**Acceptance.**
- Results table: return, Sharpe, max drawdown per strategy, with significance
  test on the difference vs baseline.
- The leak test exists and passes.
- You can explain walk-forward vs k-fold for time series, and why shuffling
  time series cross-validation is wrong.

**Interview asset.** "How do you validate a model without fooling yourself?"
Experimental design, train/val/test discipline, leakage: the unsexy stuff that
decides senior interviews.

## M6: RAG explainer over filings and transcripts

**Goal.** For any signal the engine fires, produce a cited natural-language
explanation grounded in primary documents.

**Contract.**
- Ingest a small corpus: 10-K excerpts or earnings-call transcripts from SEC
  EDGAR (free) for your tickers. Chunk, embed, store in a local vector index.
- `signal_lab.rag.explain(event)`: retrieve relevant chunks, generate an
  explanation with citations to chunk ids. Every factual claim must trace to a
  retrieved chunk; no unsupported claims.
- Evaluate on 20 events: citation precision (are cited chunks actually
  relevant?) scored by hand.

**Acceptance.**
- For the top 5 signals from M4, the explainer returns relevant cited
  passages, and you have hand-scored citation precision.
- You can explain your chunking choice, embedding choice, and how you would
  evaluate this in production (faithfulness, citation precision/recall).

**Interview asset.** This is your differentiator and mirrors LSEG Workspace
AI's Deep Research. It also turns the whole project into one crisp
walkthrough: pipeline to model to statistics to product.

## Suggested order and pacing

M1 -> M2 -> M3 -> M4 -> M5 -> M6, with M7 drills running in parallel every
day (30 minutes). If the interview lands before M6, stop and drill: M1-M5
plus drills is already a strong first round.
